# Causal planning and comprehension

Begin with a concrete everyday phenomenon that conflicts with an audience expectation. Identify where it occurs, what is surprising, why someone would continue watching and what visible action will resolve the question. Show that event before definitions or equations. Avoid unsupported sensational claims; verify facts and models internally even when the presentation is simple.

Before rendering, check whether a viewer can name the everyday puzzle without reading a title or numbers, whether every essential cause has a visible action and before/after state, whether each remaining number is necessary and attached to its conditions, and whether the ending explains the opening. Missing evidence is not a pass. A mathematical test does not establish curiosity or comprehension. Traffic and queue cases below are examples, not required topics.

Write the audience's initial expectation, observable change and revised understanding. Each shot should make one main inference possible. A shot's subject is an object or process, not a title. Preserve object identity and spatial anchors through scale changes. Include preparation, action and stable consequence; allow repetition when it supplies a controlled comparison.

Traffic example: distinguish total demand from one road's flow. Establish flow-dependent and fixed road costs before route comparisons. Label a shortcut's cost under the old split as an initial estimate, not the new equilibrium. Follow an individual reroute, then each group's contribution to shared-road flow. Show why the apparently crowded option remains preferable using complete route components. Keep other drivers fixed during a single-driver deviation. Historical cost 65 and current alternatives 80/85 are different states, not simultaneously available choices. Illustrative cars and screen travel duration are not a microscopic traffic simulation or a convergence proof.

Queue example: retain job identity, processing work and server speed. Show which queue head is taken after the same completion event; preserve idle capacity and inaccessible waiting work in the same frame. Separate total waiting, one job's waiting and batch completion time. Overall improvement need not improve every individual.

Fast pacing removes redundancy, not necessary rules or reading windows. Keep conditions beside conclusions; expose units and migration sources. Count reading time only after text is fully visible and include simultaneous new labels. A passed reading-time calculation does not prove understanding. Use independent blind explanation from actual output, then compare with the intended claim. If the mechanism is misunderstood, change the evidence, not merely font size.


## Longer explanations: density without padding

The current long-form treatment targets 180 seconds with a 20% tolerance (144–216 seconds). This is an editorial profile, not a platform requirement or a universal duration for every assignment. Add substantive visible conditions, mechanisms and comparisons; do not stretch an earlier film, repeat shots or slow playback to reach the target. Tighten redundant pauses while retaining complete reading windows and causal actions. In a narration-free treatment, a request for faster pacing does not imply adding a voice track.

A model comparison must visibly announce its change of subject before the audience mistakes it for the original everyday object splitting or transforming. Put essential applicability conditions in a readable main caption at least once, not solely in tiny corner text. Return to the familiar phenomenon and distinguish what the model explains from what it does not establish. A comparison that changes multiple conditions must not be labeled a controlled single-variable experiment.

Name the changed variable precisely. Particle shape is not material composition: a same-composition shape comparison must not be described as different ingredients. A model result is not a universal recipe for a real mixture. Use familiar descriptions before technical terms, and avoid ambiguous pronouns where two comparison groups coexist. Draw illustrative networks with appropriately weak visual links when hard connections could falsely imply chemical bonds.

When the cause is a boundary catching on a small irregularity, make the irregularity recognizable at mobile scale and show the boundary move to that visible obstacle before stopping. A sentence saying that it is blocked cannot substitute for the blocking event. Synchronize transport explanations with the tracked object's movement, rather than waiting until arrival to explain what carried it.

## Conditions, actions and result words

Audit the order of visible conditions, mechanism motion, measurable state change and conclusion text together. A result word can reveal an answer too early even when every required object eventually appears. Establish contact before transfer, reception before execution, and a distinguishable state difference before naming its relative rate or outcome. A direction arrow can explain a newly established transfer before its accumulated result is large; it cannot by itself establish a faster rate.

Keep the original object and its source context visible while an identifiable copy enters a mechanism or channel. Retain stable endpoint labels through arrival and transformation. A color match or subtitle change alone does not show where an object came from. If a diagram resets initial conditions or returns to an earlier example, label that change at the reset rather than letting apparent reverse motion imply a physical reversal.

Keep mechanism fields distinct from lighting/material shading. Clip a temperature or concentration overlay to its intended domain and give it a legend; do not let a decorative glow masquerade as transport or a quantitative solution. A stylized size-to-quantity mapping is not a conservation-law simulation. Inspect the final corrected intervals at normal speed as well as at their boundary frames.
